# Between the lines — PM day v1

Open pm-day.html in a browser. No login, server, installation, or network connection is required. Sources open externally only when clicked.

## Controls

- Play follows actual clock time from 7 AM to 5 PM. Quiet time is retained.
- Scrub to any second or jump with the chapter buttons and previous/next event controls.
- Pause to explore. Opening a record or changing a value also pauses playback.
- Reset moment restores the authored state at the current timestamp. Play restores the authored sequence.
- The micro-lecture panel is always present and advances with the day. Sources & seams and Field guide are always available.
- Ask about this moment copies or saves a context packet for a new Codex task. It does not create a task or call an AI service.
- Space plays/pauses; arrow keys move one minute when not editing a control.

## Project and evidence boundaries

This is a fictional US commercial GC project during interior rough-in. All people, companies, record IDs, project names, amounts, geometry, observations and timestamps are authored. No actual PM screen recording or real project records are reproduced. The supplied Tyler call was inspiration, not instructions or verified industry-wide evidence. The 30-minute site walk is 5% of this ten-hour demonstration only.

The product names and selected record concepts are real. UI layouts are simplified educational reconstructions. Excel is a custom PM workbook, not a standard industry template. The look-ahead, player and scenario controls are authored teaching interfaces.

## Consistent record chain

- RFI #087: Level 2 west corridor C/4 supply duct / beam conflict. References M2.12 rev 2, S2.02 rev 1, Clash 014.
- Friday report: 9 Summit workers × 8 hours = 72 labor hours. Other trades: 264 hours. Total 336 hours, 42 people. Attendance does not prove installed quantity.
- RFI response becomes available at 10:55 AM; commercial authorization remains separate.
- CE #024 appears at 11:15 AM with an $8,000 ROM.
- Q-119 arrives 12:10 PM: labor $4,800 + material $3,200 + equipment $800 + markup $800 = $9,600. One extra workday is requested, not accepted.
- The PM updates the shadow forecast at 12:40 PM; the selected official budget view carries $9,600 at 1:15 PM. The quote replaces the ROM, avoiding double counting.
- SC-023 stays $420,000. Selected projected budget is $438,000. Forecast after quote = $429,600. Projected over/under = $8,400. This is not whole-project profit.
- Budget mapping assumption: open CE estimates feed Pending Cost Changes; Direct Costs and additional Forecast to Complete are zero in this selected example. Actual Procore configurations vary.
- RTU assumed release September 18 + 56 calendar days = November 13; required November 9. Release is unconfirmed.
- West rough-in baseline September 14–16; inspection September 17; ceiling closure September 18. Scenario uses a Monday–Friday calendar. Local exposure does not establish a project critical-path delay.

## Timestamp index

### 1. 7:00 AM — Before the first call

Application: mail. View: morning.

Action: Read the overnight mail.

The building is already moving. Maya starts by finding what changed since Friday, not by reading every message in order.

Known then: The ceiling conflict was reported Friday. No design response has arrived.

PM question: What needs an answer before a crew loses its work front?

Teaching cue: Open the superintendent’s message. A work front is an area where a crew can work without another constraint stopping it.

Source seam: Authored day and correspondence. The office-first rhythm is informed by practitioner accounts.

### 2. 7:12 AM — Yesterday is evidence

Application: procore. View: Daily Log.

Action: Review Friday’s daily log.

Nine sheet-metal workers were on site. That says who attended; it does not prove how much duct they installed. Maya compares the note with the planned work.

Known then: Friday’s report records a blocked area at Level 2, grid C/4.

PM question: Does manpower tell us progress?

Teaching cue: Open the manpower entry and the delay note. Daily logs are reports from people, not a live scan of the building.

Source seam: Procore tool and log concepts are borrowed. Entries and quantities are fictional.

### 3. 7:30 AM — Find the dependency

Application: schedule. View: lookahead.

Action: Compare the two-week look-ahead.

Duct rough-in must finish before the above-ceiling inspection. The superintendent can move the crew to the east wing today, but the west corridor still needs a solution.

Known then: West rough-in is planned for September 14–16; inspection is booked for September 17.

PM question: Which date is exposed—and which date is not yet proven to move?

Teaching cue: Select activity M-240. A look-ahead is a near-term work plan; it is not a full critical-path analysis.

Source seam: Illustrative look-ahead, not a Primavera P6 or Microsoft Project screen. No critical-path claim.

### 4. 7:50 AM — Who has the ball?

Application: procore. View: RFIs.

Action: Follow up RFI #087.

The open question belongs with the design team. Maya asks for a response today and explains the inspection dependency, without directing the subcontractor to improvise a design.

Known then: RFI #087 is Open. Elena Park, the mechanical engineer, is the assignee.

PM question: Who must act next?

Teaching cue: Ball In Court identifies who owes the next action. RFI means Request for Information.

Source seam: Real Procore field names, reconstructed record. Due dates are this project’s assumptions.

### 5. 8:15 AM — See the conflict

Application: navis. View: clash.

Action: Join the VDC screen-share.

Luis, the VDC coordinator, shares a Navisworks Manage view: supply duct meets a structural beam. Maya needs the location, affected trade, and decision—not a model-editing session.

Known then: The coordinated models show a 3-inch overlap. RFI #087 asks for a viable route.

PM question: Is this a geometry problem, an installation fact, or both?

Teaching cue: Select Clash 014. A detected clash is model evidence; the field team separately confirms the location.

Source seam: Simplified schematic geometry. Clash Detective terminology is borrowed; this is not a BIM model.

### 6. 8:40 AM — Check the promise

Application: revit. View: sheet.

Action: Review the designer’s sheet and schedule.

The engineer shares Revit to locate the duct and check the design information. The issued sheet is the reference for this question; the working model alone does not settle contractual precedence.

Known then: Issued M2.12 revision 2 shows the west corridor route. The structural beam is on S2.02.

PM question: Which revision is the team actually building from?

Teaching cue: Switch between the sheet and Duct Schedule. In Revit, a schedule can be a table of model elements, not a calendar.

Source seam: Revit Project Browser / Sheets / Schedules grammar is borrowed. Design dimensions are illustrative.

### 7. 9:00 AM — Now, the waiting

Application: mail. View: followup.

Action: Document the call and wait for design.

Maya sends a concise recap: exact location, affected inspection, and who owes what. The crew works the east wing. An unanswered email is still an unresolved dependency.

Known then: A response is requested by noon. No approved reroute exists yet.

PM question: What can proceed while this answer is outstanding?

Teaching cue: The full timeline includes waiting. Scrub to another event whenever you like; playback remains one second per second.

Source seam: Invented timing. Nothing is automatically escalated by a hidden prediction engine.

### 8. 9:25 AM — A second clock is ticking

Application: procore. View: Submittals.

Action: Review the rooftop-unit submittal.

The rooftop-unit product data came back Revise and Resubmit. A long lead item can miss the required-on-site date even when nobody on site is waiting for it today.

Known then: Submittal 23 74 00-1 revision 1 needs corrected electrical data. Release is not confirmed.

PM question: Is approval complete enough to release fabrication?

Teaching cue: A submittal presents product or shop-drawing information for review. An RFI asks a question. They are different records.

Source seam: Fictional procurement thread; response labels follow common Procore practice and are configurable.

### 9. 9:55 AM — The shadow sheet

Application: excel. View: procurement.

Action: Check the procurement tracker.

The official review record lives in Procore. Maya’s workbook joins it to the supplier’s lead time and the required-on-site date. The spreadsheet is useful precisely because someone maintains those links.

Known then: Assumed release September 18 plus 56 calendar days gives November 13; required on site November 9.

PM question: What assumption is hidden in a delivery date?

Teaching cue: Edit the lead-time cell. The date uses calendar days here, explicitly—not working days.

Source seam: Custom Excel workbook. The formula is simulated in the browser; no live workbook connection.

### 10. 10:25 AM — Leave the desk

Application: site. View: walk.

Action: Walk Level 2 with the superintendent.

Maya and Rafael confirm the affected corridor and the east-wing work front. The walk supplies context that a colored model cannot. Luis keeps responsibility for model coordination.

Known then: No west-corridor reroute has been installed. The east-wing crew can continue today.

PM question: What did we observe, and what are we still assuming?

Teaching cue: Open the field notes. This 30-minute walk is 5% of this ten-hour demo, not a claim about all PMs.

Source seam: Schematic field illustration and invented observation notes; no site photograph or scan.

### 11. 10:55 AM — An answer is not an order

Application: mail. View: response.

Action: Read the engineer’s RFI response.

The engineer permits a lower route subject to the stated coordination conditions. Maya still needs trade pricing, installation duration, and the project’s authorization process.

Known then: The response is now available. Added cost and schedule impact remain to be evaluated.

PM question: Did a design answer also authorize extra money?

Teaching cue: Open the RFI response. Separate technical direction, commercial approval, and field execution.

Source seam: Fictional engineering response for education; not construction direction.

### 12. 11:15 AM — Capture the exposure

Application: procore. View: Change Events.

Action: Create Change Event #024.

Maya records the potential change against the affected subcontract. A change event tracks exposure; it does not mean the owner approved payment or the subcontract amount changed.

Known then: CE #024 is Open. The working cost ROM is $8,000; scope is marked TBD.

PM question: Where should an unpriced change live?

Teaching cue: ROM means Rough Order of Magnitude. It is an estimate, not the subcontractor’s final price.

Source seam: Procore change-event concepts are borrowed. Configuration, IDs, and cost codes are chosen for this demo.

### 13. 11:40 AM — A quiet half hour

Application: mail. View: lunch.

Action: Lunch, voicemail, routine correspondence.

The quote is still outstanding. Nothing in the records proves the west corridor is back on plan. Maya uses the gap for routine email and lunch.

Known then: No quotation received yet. Today’s reroute authorization remains outstanding.

PM question: Does no new information mean the risk went away?

Teaching cue: You can jump directly to 12:10 PM for the quote, or explore any record available now.

Source seam: Authored quiet interval. No new project evidence arrives in this segment.

### 14. 12:10 PM — Put a price on the answer

Application: mail. View: quote.

Action: Read Summit Mechanical’s quotation.

The subcontractor prices the reroute at $9,600 and asks for one additional workday. Maya checks the breakdown and whether the claimed time actually affects the inspection.

Known then: Q-119: $4,800 labor + $3,200 material + $800 equipment + $800 markup = $9,600.

PM question: What is priced, and what still needs checking?

Teaching cue: Open Q-119. A quoted day of trade effort is not automatically a day added to project completion.

Source seam: Invented quote with explicit arithmetic. No owner approval is implied.

### 15. 12:40 PM — Two numbers, one obligation

Application: excel. View: exposure.

Action: Reconcile the cost forecast.

Maya replaces the $8,000 placeholder with the $9,600 quote. Keeping both would count the same exposure twice. The subcontract remains $420,000 until an approved change changes it.

Known then: Mechanical projected budget $438,000; forecast $429,600; remaining headroom $8,400.

PM question: Why does the forecast move before the commitment?

Teaching cue: Edit the quote amount. The workbook shows a selected scope only, not the total project budget.

Source seam: Custom shadow-sheet logic. The simplified budget excludes other mechanical exposures in this fictional scope.

### 16. 1:15 PM — Bring the record up to date

Application: procore. View: Budget.

Action: Review the selected budget line.

The CE cost estimate is updated to $9,600. In this demo’s chosen budget configuration it appears in Pending Cost Changes. The projected over/under is $8,400.

Known then: No approved commitment change order exists. Owner recovery remains uncertain.

PM question: Are we forecasting a cost or authorizing it?

Teaching cue: Open the budget calculation. Procore budget columns and change-order tiers vary by company configuration.

Source seam: Real budget column names; simplified, explicitly configured source mapping.

### 17. 1:45 PM — Give the delivery somewhere to go

Application: cmb. View: logistics.

Action: Review cmBuilder with the superintendent.

A separate delivery needs the west gate tomorrow. The logistics scenario tests where a truck and temporary laydown can fit while keeping the planned access route clear.

Known then: Tomorrow’s logistics plan moves laydown east. The superintendent still needs to confirm the area is clear.

PM question: Does a site plan prove the area will be available?

Teaching cue: Switch Milestones. A scenario is a planning alternative, not live evidence of equipment locations.

Source seam: cmBuilder Scenario / Milestone / Resources language. Flat schematic, no validated clearance or lifting analysis.

### 18. 2:20 PM — Protect the inspection

Application: schedule. View: risk.

Action: Test the one-day impact.

If west rough-in finishes September 17, the inspection booked for that day needs coordination. Maya records an exposed handoff and asks Rafael to confirm resequencing, rather than declaring a project delay.

Known then: Trade duration is claimed, not accepted. The baseline stays visible beside the scenario.

PM question: Can resequencing preserve the inspection window?

Teaching cue: Use the scenario control. This is a deterministic illustration of local dependencies, not a probability forecast.

Source seam: Proposed teaching interaction; not native Procore, P6, or Navisworks functionality.

### 19. 3:00 PM — Say what is known

Application: mail. View: owner.

Action: Prepare the owner update.

The update separates design resolution, pending price, and schedule exposure. Maya asks for a decision through the agreed change process and names the next owner of each action.

Known then: $9,600 proposed; inspection at risk; no approved change order, no verified project-completion delay.

PM question: Can the owner tell the difference between fact and forecast?

Teaching cue: Open the update. Notice “pending” and “subject to confirmation”—these are consequential distinctions.

Source seam: Authored communication. It is not sent anywhere.

### 20. 3:50 PM — Leave tomorrow a clean trail

Application: procore. View: Closeout.

Action: Review the handoff list.

The day ends with a technical answer, a better cost forecast, and unresolved approvals. Maya assigns follow-ups so the next person can act without reconstructing the entire day.

Known then: Commercial approval, inspection confirmation, revised coordination check, and RTU resubmittal remain open.

PM question: What is still open at 5 PM?

Teaching cue: Check off items in your exploration. Reset restores the recorded handoff; the scenario does not pretend every problem is solved.

Source seam: Fictional end-of-day handoff. Interactive checklist changes do not update Procore.

### 21. 5:00 PM — The day is done. The work is not.

Application: procore. View: Closeout.

Action: End of the recorded day.

Maya has reduced uncertainty. The building itself is not magically caught up: an answer is documented, an exposure is priced, and tomorrow’s responsibilities are clear.

Known then: The pending $9,600 exposure is visible. Approval and inspection follow-up carry into tomorrow.

PM question: Which uncertainty did each tool help remove?

Teaching cue: Scrub backward to compare what Maya knew in the morning with what she knew at close.
